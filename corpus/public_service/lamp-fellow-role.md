## The main job of a LAMP Fellow

A LAMP Fellow is mentored by a Member of Parliament (MP) for about 10 to 11 months, depending on the Parliament calendar. The fellow's main job is "providing research support to the Member of Parliament (MP)."

## The work a LAMP Fellow does

Much of the work is about Parliament: input on bills, parliamentary questions, policy discussions and standing committee meetings. Fellows also prepare background research for conferences and media events the MP attends. Typical tasks include:

- legislative and data analysis
- drafting parliamentary questions and Private Member Bills
- preparing background for debates and committee meetings
- media work, such as press releases and preparing for TV appearances
- research on issues in the MP's constituency and meeting people affected by them

Research topics can include defence, food security, environment, economics and foreign affairs.

## What a LAMP Fellow's day is like

The page says fellows rarely have a "typical" work day. Deadlines can range "from thirty minutes to a couple of days", and the workload is heavier when Parliament is in session. Work in the MP's constituency depends on what the MP and the fellow agree.
